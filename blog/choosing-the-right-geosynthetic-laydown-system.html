Choosing the Right Geosynthetic Laydown System for Your Crew

Not every job needs a Grizzly 600T. Learn how to match laydown equipment to your roll widths, site constraints, and crew size—from municipal maintenance to DOT highway projects.

Understanding Your Project Requirements

The first step in choosing the right geosynthetic laydown system isn't browsing spec sheets—it's understanding the actual conditions your crew will face on the ground. A system that works perfectly for highway paving might be overkill for municipal maintenance, and vice versa.

Roll Width and Material Type

The width of your geosynthetic rolls dictates your equipment's required reach. Standard paving fabrics typically come in 4 ft to 15 ft widths, but some industrial applications use rolls up to 20 ft wide. The Grizzly 600T handles the full range with telescoping extensions, while the Grizzly Cub 300T is optimized for narrower municipal rolls.

  • Standard highway fabric (12–15 ft): Grizzly 600T with standard telescoping arms
  • Municipal/maintenance rolls (4–10 ft): Grizzly Cub 300T for lighter footprint
  • Specialty wide rolls (15–20 ft): Grizzly 600T with brush extensions
  • Grid or mat systems: Custom mounting kits available for both platforms

Site Constraints Matter

Open highway work gives you room to maneuver, but urban resurfacing, parking lots, and airfield maintenance often mean tight quarters. The swing-arm systems on both Grizzly models let you finish within six inches of curbs, guardrails, and walls without repositioning the tractor.

If you're regularly working around obstacles or in confined spaces, the articulating brush mounts and crab-claw swing arms become essential—not luxury features.

Crew Size and Labor Economics

Labor availability is the silent variable that determines equipment ROI. Traditional bolt-together laydown systems need five or six people to manage roll handling, tension control, and brushing. Modern telescoping systems like the Grizzly cut that to three operators.

The Three-Person Standard

With proper equipment, a three-person crew can execute professional geosynthetic installs at highway speeds:

  • Operator 1: Tractor driver managing forward progress and alignment
  • Operator 2: Monitoring fabric tension and brush contact from the ground
  • Operator 3: Managing roll changes and handling seam overlaps

This crew structure works because the laydown system automates tension control, brush articulation, and edge positioning—tasks that used to require dedicated personnel.

Budget Considerations

The Grizzly 600T represents the full-featured platform with dual-bar telescoping, short-roll arms, and all upgrade paths available. The Grizzly Cub 300T offers the core innovation at a lower price point for crews who don't need maximum reach or heavy-duty components.

Total Cost of Ownership

When calculating equipment costs, factor in more than the purchase price:

  • Labor savings: Smaller crew = lower daily operating costs
  • Rework reduction: Better tension control = fewer wrinkle repairs
  • Speed gains: Faster installs = more tonnage per shift
  • Maintenance costs: One-piece welded frames need less service than bolt-together rigs

Most crews find that labor savings alone justify the investment within the first season of heavy use.

Decision Framework

Use this quick framework to narrow your options:

Choose Grizzly 600T if:

  • You're running wide rolls (12 ft+) regularly
  • Highway, DOT, or airport work is your primary market
  • Short-roll installation (1–10 ft widths) is part of your scope
  • You need oil-truck mounting for combined oil + fabric passes
  • Future upgrade paths (hydraulics, automated tension) are important

Choose Grizzly Cub 300T if:

  • Municipal maintenance and parking lot work dominate your schedule
  • Roll widths stay in the 4–10 ft range
  • You value lighter weight and easier transport between jobs
  • Budget constraints favor a lower entry point with proven technology

Consider Custom Builds if:

  • Landfill or industrial applications require non-standard widths
  • Unique mounting requirements (cable systems, hover heads, multi-roll rigs)
  • Specialized material handling (heavy grids, composite mats, etc.)
